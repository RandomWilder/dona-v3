## Rendered on staging at 375px

Driven against the deployed origin, not localhost:

- `document.fonts` reports **`Heebo: loaded`** twice (Hebrew and Latin subsets) and
  `document.fonts.check('400 14px Heebo') === true` — the pages render in our own
  self-hosted face, not a system fallback. IBM Plex Mono stays `unloaded` because
  nothing uses `.mono` yet; that is `font-display: swap` behaving correctly.
- `document.documentElement.dir === 'rtl'`, and the ops rail sits on the **inline-start**
  edge, which is the right.
- The drawer opens on the ≤839px layout and lists all seven destinations in order:
  `queue · conversations · approvals · reports · properties · people · guidance`.
- The tenant composer: `textarea.disabled && button.disabled === true`.

## The security claims, tested rather than asserted

- **No path ever comes from the request.** `/ui/fonts/:file` is matched against a fixed
  `Set`; `../` and percent-encoded traversal attempts 404 (`assets.test.ts`).
- **`/t/:link` never reflects its parameter.** On staging,
  `curl '.../t/%3Cscript%3Ealert(1)%3C%2Fscript%3E' | grep -c 'alert(1)'` → **0**.
- **No third-party request.** Fonts are served from our origin; the token guard fails any
  shell containing `fonts.googleapis`.

## Why a broken asset cannot reach a phone quietly

`registerUiAssets` reads `tokens.css` and all four faces at **registration**, inside
`buildApp`, which `boot.ts` runs before `app.listen()`. A file missing from the container
image throws before the process serves anything, so the revision never becomes ready and
`infra/smoke.sh` fails the deploy. There is no path where the shell ships with fallback
fonts and a green pipeline. This is why `smoke.sh` did not need a new UI assertion.

## Carried out of this slice

Fastify's own 404 echoes the requested path and its 500 carries the thrown message. Both
now render through `toErrorBody` — the same rule as `/health`, applied at the edge. The
remaining gap is recorded in `SPEC-kernel.md`: schema-validation failures must map to
`invalid` (400), not `unavailable` (503), and that must be closed by the first route that
takes a body.
